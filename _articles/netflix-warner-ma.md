*The $30 Paramount offer creates a credible challenge to Netflix's signed agreement. The deciding variable is the value of the assets WBD shareholders retain, adjusted for financing, timing and completion risk.*

## Transaction view

Netflix's agreement to acquire Warner Bros.' studios and streaming assets remains a viable transaction despite Paramount Skydance's competing $30-per-share tender offer. However, the existing agreement should not be assigned a decisive advantage merely because it was signed first.

The competing proposals acquire different assets. Netflix's $27.75-per-share consideration excludes Discovery Global, which WBD shareholders would retain following the planned separation. Paramount proposes to acquire the entire company for cash. Comparing the headline prices without valuing the retained business would produce a misleading conclusion.

Our assessment is that Netflix retains a plausible advantage on risk-adjusted value, but Paramount's financing commitments and willingness to pursue shareholders directly make the outcome less certain than the existing merger agreement suggests.

Discovery Global must be worth more than approximately **$2.25 per existing WBD share**, assuming Netflix's $27.75 consideration remains intact, for its aggregate nominal package to exceed Paramount's $30 offer. That threshold is not an estimate of intrinsic value; it identifies the valuation question shareholders must resolve.

The more difficult issue is whether the separated networks business can sustain the debt allocated to it. EBITDA alone is inadequate without assessing cash interest, declining linear television revenue, restructuring costs and maturities. Paramount's all-cash proposal removes stock-market exposure from its consideration but not financing or regulatory risk. Its December 8 filing identifies financing commitments and equity backing, so it cannot be dismissed as merely indicative.

## Why Netflix's industrial logic matters

Netflix's acquisition rationale combines distribution scale and content ownership. Management expects $2–3 billion in annual cost savings by the third year, but those savings should not be capitalized as though immediately available. At a 10% discount rate, a hypothetical $2.5 billion perpetual annual pre-tax saving has a headline present value of $25 billion **if available immediately**. This is not an acquisition valuation: taxes, implementation costs, delays and revenue dis-synergies would reduce its economic value.

The implication is that Netflix may have buyer-specific synergies unavailable to an independent owner. The same integration raises competition and execution questions, particularly where content ownership and distribution overlap.

## Completion outlook

The existing agreement provides Netflix with a defined structure, while Paramount's proposal could become more attractive if financing and closing terms improve. Critical variables are Discovery Global's capitalization and standalone value, changes to consideration, financing protections, termination provisions and regulatory milestones.

Our working view favors Netflix's agreement remaining the principal completion route. The risk is not simply a higher competing price but a revised Paramount proposal that improves both consideration and perceived certainty.

**Transaction conclusion:** Netflix's signed agreement has meaningful value, but its completion case depends on whether retained Discovery Global equity and execution advantages outweigh Paramount's cash premium.
