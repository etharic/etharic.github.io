*Inspired by Things 3. Guided by Getting Things Done. Built through vibe coding.*

## Why I built it

I built **Clearline** to make everyday life more intentional: one place to capture ideas, clarify commitments, and focus on what matters. I admired the calm, considered design of **Things 3**, but its Apple-only ecosystem did not fit my life. My iPhone is my main device; I also use Windows and want access from a Mac or other modern devices.

Instead of changing my workflow to fit a product, I built a product around my workflow. **Clearline is genuinely a vibe-coded project**: through AI-assisted coding, testing, and iteration, I created personal software without commissioning a development team. The ability to shape my own tools is itself a meaningful addition to my life.

## The principles

Clearline draws on **David Allen's *Getting Things Done* (GTD)**: the mind is better used for thinking than for repeatedly remembering unfinished obligations. Its five stages are **capture, clarify, organize, reflect, and engage**.

I want a trusted external brain—not an ever-growing list that creates more stress. Capture thoughts quickly, turn vague ideas into next actions, place them in the right context, review regularly, and then do the work.

I put ideas, errands, reminders, and obligations here the moment they occur. Organization can wait; forgetting should not.

## The value in my life

Clearline gives me **mental freedom** by moving commitments out of my head, **device freedom** by reducing dependence on one ecosystem, and **creative freedom** through vibe coding. I built it without conventional development fees, using lightweight infrastructure designed to avoid subscriptions and routine server maintenance. Hosting limits, software updates, and occasional troubleshooting can still apply.

I borrowed the inspiration from Things 3, adopted the discipline of GTD, and built something for my own life. **Less mental clutter. More control. More attention for what matters.**
