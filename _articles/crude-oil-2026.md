*Inventory accumulation and production growth support a softer Brent outlook. The principal risk is that a concentrated disruption to deliverable supply overwhelms the global surplus.*

## Sector view

The global oil market enters 2026 with a substantial inventory buffer and projected production growth exceeding incremental consumption. These fundamentals support a cautious view on crude prices and upstream earnings. Our central scenario places Brent predominantly within **$55–65/bbl in H2 2026**, assuming no prolonged interruption to major export routes. This is an independent scenario, not an IEA forecast or a statistical confidence interval.

The distinction is between a well-supplied market and a market immune to disruption. The former is supported by available evidence; the latter is not.

## Physical balance and inventories

The [IEA January Oil Market Report](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-january-2026) forecasts supply growth of 2.5 million barrels per day against demand growth of 0.93 million barrels per day. Observed inventories increased substantially in 2025, indicating that excess production was already entering storage.

**Exhibit 1. Supply-side indicators**

| Indicator | January 2026 evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Forecast 2026 supply growth | +2.50 mb/d |
| Forecast 2026 demand growth | +0.93 mb/d |
| Difference in annual growth | +1.57 mb/d |
| Observed 2025 stock accumulation | ~470 million barrels |
| December North Sea Dated average | $62.64/bbl |

*Source: IEA January 21, 2026. Difference in growth rates is not the projected absolute surplus.*

Incremental supply is likely to compete for buyers rather than meet an immediate shortage. Inventories can absorb temporary demand increases, reducing the need for rapid price appreciation. However, global stock totals conceal differences in location, crude quality and accessibility. Commercial stocks, strategic reserves and oil in transit are not interchangeable when a disruption occurs at a particular refinery or shipping route.

## Implications for upstream earnings

For producers, the main transmission mechanism is realized prices and operating cash generation. Production may remain stable over shorter periods because existing wells can operate even when lower prices discourage new investment.

**Exhibit 2. Illustrative upstream price sensitivity**

*Hypothetical producer: 250,000 barrels/day; US$ billion annually.*

| Realized oil price | $50/bbl | $60/bbl | $70/bbl |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gross revenue | 4.56 | 5.48 | 6.39 |
| Operating contribution at $30/bbl unit cost | 1.83 | 2.74 | 3.65 |

*Illustrative constant-volume scenario. Excludes royalties, taxes, hedging, transport differentials, corporate expenses and capital expenditure. Operating contribution is not free cash flow.*

Every $10/bbl movement in realized price changes annual gross revenue by approximately $913 million for this production base. Low-cost operators may preserve investment capacity at prices that force higher-cost producers to reduce drilling, eventually limiting supply. The response is delayed because existing-well production depends on operating economics, not the full cost of developing replacement reserves.

## Scenarios and invalidation

**Exhibit 3. Brent scenario framework**

| Scenario | H2 2026 range | Economic condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Downside | $45–55 | Larger stock builds; limited production restraint |
| Central | $55–65 | Surplus persists; export routes remain operational |
| Upside | $70–90 | Material supply restraint or regional disruption |

*Independent scenario ranges, not probability-weighted forecasts. A severe shock could exceed the illustrated upside range.*

The central case is supported by production growth and accumulated inventories. The main counterargument is that the physical market can tighten rapidly when losses are geographically concentrated. A disruption affecting several million barrels per day of export capacity could exceed the projected incremental surplus. Tanker interruptions can also create shortages of deliverable crude without destroying equivalent production capacity.

The forecast requires reassessment if inventories begin declining persistently, physical crude differentials strengthen or export availability deteriorates.

## Sector conclusion

January evidence supports a softer oil-price environment, with implications for upstream cash generation and high-cost investment. The weakness of the forecast is its reliance on continued access to major production and transport routes. Inventories cushion modest interruptions, but their effectiveness depends on location and deliverability.

**Central expectation:** Brent trades predominantly within $55–65 in H2 2026, conditional on the absence of a sustained major supply disruption.
