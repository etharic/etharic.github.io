*The FY2025 guidance reset points to weaker commercial momentum and increasing earnings risk. Valuation has become less demanding, but further estimate revisions remain a concern.*

## Investment view

**Cautious**

Novo Nordisk’s latest guidance reduction reflects a deterioration in US commercial expectations, with competition and net pricing pressure weighing on the earnings outlook. Our illustrative FY2025 estimates sit modestly below management’s revised guidance midpoints. Although the share-price correction has compressed valuation multiples, limited earnings visibility argues against interpreting the decline as an immediate re-rating opportunity.

**Key estimates:** FY25E sales growth 10.5% (CER); FY25E EBIT growth 11.5% (CER); FY25E EBIT DKK 143bn (illustrative CER basis).

## FY2025 guidance reset

Novo Nordisk’s [July 29 trading update](https://www.novonordisk.com/news-and-media/news-and-ir-materials/news-details.html?id=916407) reduced full-year sales-growth guidance from 13–21% to 8–14% and operating-profit growth guidance from 16–24% to 10–16%, both at constant exchange rates (CER). Management attributed the downgrade primarily to weaker US expectations for Wegovy and Ozempic, continued competition from compounded GLP-1 products and slower-than-anticipated international uptake.

*Source: Novo Nordisk FY2024 results and July 29, 2025 guidance. Independent illustrative estimates use FY2024 reported sales of DKK 290.4bn and EBIT of DKK 128.3bn as the reference base. CER-derived amounts are not reported-currency forecasts.*

Our estimates imply approximately DKK 19bn of sales and DKK 11bn of EBIT downside relative to the previous guidance midpoint. The revision is significant for a company whose valuation previously reflected sustained double-digit growth and high operating margins.

Foreign exchange presents an additional headwind. Management expects reported-currency sales growth to be approximately four percentage points below CER guidance, with operating-profit growth around seven points lower. This translation effect should be separated from the underlying deterioration in commercial performance.

## Competitive dynamics and earnings sensitivity

The investment debate is shifting from obesity-treatment market growth to Novo’s ability to defend market share and net realized prices. Eli Lilly’s competing treatments have increased competitive intensity in the US, while patient access, product availability and payer negotiations remain important determinants of commercial performance.

*Independent scenario analysis. Central sales assumption rounded from DKK 320.9bn. The analyst’s illustrative EBIT estimate of DKK 143.1bn implies a 44.6% margin.*

The principal earnings risk is that volume growth fails to offset pricing pressure and higher commercial expenditure. Manufacturing efficiencies and favorable product mix could provide some mitigation, although sustained margin recovery will depend on the company’s ability to stabilize its competitive position.

At the central revenue assumption, a 100-basis-point change in operating margin represents approximately DKK 3.2bn of annual EBIT. The sensitivity highlights the importance of net pricing and cost discipline as competition increases. Even modest margin compression could materially affect earnings if volume growth remains below previous expectations.

## Valuation and earnings risk

Novo’s share-price correction has reduced its historical valuation premium, but the appropriate multiple remains dependent on earnings visibility and the durability of its competitive position. Further downward revisions could offset part of the apparent valuation benefit.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

*Illustrative valuation scenarios, not formal price targets. EPS assumptions are independent sensitivities rather than modeled reported-currency forecasts. FY2024 diluted EPS was DKK 22.63.*

The sensitivity underscores the combined effect of earnings revisions and multiple compression. A sustained re-rating would require improved confidence in Novo’s forward earnings trajectory, particularly its ability to protect profitability as competition intensifies.

The company’s established manufacturing capabilities, product portfolio and international distribution remain important competitive assets. However, these strengths alone do not establish that the current valuation adequately reflects further downside risk.

## Outlook and catalysts

The near-term outlook remains cautious, with US prescription trends and realized pricing likely to drive subsequent estimate revisions. Stabilization in Wegovy and Ozempic demand, improved payer access and more consistent commercial execution would support greater confidence in management’s revised outlook.

The principal downside risks are further market-share losses, higher rebates and weaker operating leverage. Conversely, stronger prescription momentum, improved product availability and successful pipeline execution could support an earnings recovery and renewed multiple expansion.

Our illustrative FY2025 estimates of DKK 321bn in sales and DKK 143bn in EBIT remain modestly below management’s revised midpoints on a CER-derived basis. Until there is clearer evidence of earnings stabilization, the recent share-price correction alone does not justify a more constructive valuation stance.

The central investment question is no longer the size of the obesity-treatment market, but Novo Nordisk’s ability to defend profitable growth within it.
