*The connectivity business provides a measurable operating foundation, but the consolidated valuation reflects expectations extending beyond demonstrated earnings.*

## Investment view

SpaceX's proposed public offering should attract substantial institutional demand, supported by Starlink's commercial scale, launch capabilities and the limited availability of comparable listed assets. Our expectation is that the offering can support an equity valuation **exceeding $1.5 trillion**. This forecasts public-market demand, not intrinsic value.

SpaceX's profitable connectivity operations coexist with capital-intensive activities whose economic returns remain uncertain. Treating the company as a pure satellite-broadband business would misrepresent the investment.

## Segment economics

The May 2026 IPO disclosures provide a clearer basis for distinguishing the operating businesses. Based on [contemporary Reuters reporting on the filing](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/bound-for-mars-elon-musks-spacex-unveils-filing-for-blockbuster-ipo-4702463), connectivity generated approximately $3.26 billion of first-quarter revenue and $1.19 billion of segment operating profit.

Connectivity's approximately 36.5% segment operating margin is meaningful, but it is not a free-cash-flow margin. Satellites require replacement; network capacity, customer equipment and launch infrastructure require continuing investment. The advantage of vertically integrated launches depends on lifecycle cost and return on invested capital, not merely lower apparent deployment expense.

The question is whether incremental subscribers and network capacity generate attractive returns after accounting for replacement cycles and investment requirements.

## Valuation framework

Using approximately $18.7 billion of FY2025 consolidated revenue, a $1.5 trillion equity valuation implies approximately 80× historical sales.

**Exhibit 2. Equity valuation sensitivity**

| Equity valuation | Equity value / FY2025 sales |
| --- | --- |
| $1.25tn | ~67× |
| $1.50tn | ~80× |
| $1.75tn | ~94× |
| $2.00tn | ~107× |

*Illustrative equity-value-to-sales ratios, not enterprise-value multiples or fair-value estimates.*

These multiples cannot be justified by consolidated historical revenue alone. Investors must anticipate substantial growth, strong incremental margins or significant value from activities that have not established comparable earnings. A sum-of-the-parts valuation would distinguish mature connectivity cash flows from launch operations and higher-risk development activities. But reliable segment capex, corporate allocations and long-term reinvestment assumptions are needed before assigning a defensible intrinsic value.

## Capital allocation and governance

The group reported substantial first-quarter capital expenditure, including investment associated with the AI business. This creates a potential divergence between operating value creation and consolidated cash generation: a profitable segment may finance expansion elsewhere, without that expenditure immediately earning attractive returns.

Investors should monitor capital expenditure by business, operating cash conversion, debt requirements and how connectivity profits are allocated. Founder control further limits minority shareholders' influence over capital allocation. Such control may support long-horizon projects, but it also increases the importance of evaluating governance and reinvestment discipline.

## IPO outlook

We expect Starlink's established commercial base, integrated launch infrastructure and scarcity value to support institutional demand for the offering. Risks include valuation sensitivity, capital intensity and uncertain returns from non-connectivity activities.

The forecast does not require near-term consolidated profitability. It assumes investors will capitalize future growth and strategic position at an exceptional valuation.

**A successful IPO would establish willingness to pay for SpaceX's future earnings potential, not that the offering valuation is fundamentally inexpensive or that subsequent shareholder returns will be attractive.**
